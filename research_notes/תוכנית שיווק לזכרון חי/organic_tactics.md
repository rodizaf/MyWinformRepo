# Organic Growth Tactics for an AI Photo Restoration / Animation Micro-Business (Zikaron Chai), and What to Avoid

Scope note: findings come from web search result summaries (2021-2026). Full-page fetches failed for www.ctvnews.ca and www.aol.com because the session network blocks those hosts, so the claims attributed to those two sources come from search snippets only. Vendor blogs (CapCut, VanceAI, Wondershare, giveaway-tool companies) are flagged where used, because they have a commercial interest in the trends they describe.

## 1. Which content formats perform best for restoration/animation accounts, with concrete viral examples

### Takeaway
The proven viral pattern in this niche is "a real person's photo, one emotional relationship (parent, grandparent, spouse), one transformation moment": the still image comes to life, someone hugs a dead parent, or a damaged photo is restored. The largest documented hits (Deep Nostalgia 2021, the AI hug trend in 2024, Alexis Ohanian's mother video in 2025, the Gemini "polaroid with a late loved one" trend in 2025) all ran on this pattern, and all of them drew both tears and an "it's creepy" backlash in the comments. Process time-lapses with a story caption (the SMPrestores model) are the documented format for a restoration service business that wins clients.

- CapCut recommends a before/after contrast as an opening hook formula (vendor advice, not evidence). — [CapCut hooks guide](https://www.capcut.com/create/short-form-video-hooks)

### Inferences
Format playbook, ranked by evidence strength:
1. **"Still to alive" reveal (0-2s).** Open on the damaged or black-and-white still, then cut to the moment it moves (a blink, a smile, a turn to camera). This is the Deep Nostalgia mechanic, and it shows the transformation in the first frame without needing a "watch till the end" line. Give the first frame text in Hebrew that names the relationship: "סבתא שלי, 1952. אף פעם לא ראיתי אותה זזה."
2. **Relationship-first caption storytelling (Ohanian model).** One sentence of real memory, such as "this is how she used to hug me", does more than any description of the technology. Put the story first and keep the tech in the background.
3. **Restoration time-lapse or slider (SMPrestores model).** Slow, satisfying repair of tears and stains, then the colorized version, with a short caption giving the backstory. This is the format with a documented path from views to paying clients.
4. **Reaction-of-the-recipient videos** (section 2), the highest-emotion format. Lakyn Bowman (8.5M) and the Deep Nostalgia parent clips show that the person reacting is the content.
5. **Trend-jacking**: hug videos, "photo with my late grandfather" and similar. Ride the trend when one appears, but label the result as AI (section 6) and avoid the creepier outputs (section 5).
6. **Non-grief variety, to avoid being "the death account"**: animated wedding photos for anniversaries, a child's drawing brought to life, pets, parents as children, olim and immigrants' arrival photos. These widen the gift occasions (birthdays, anniversaries, Mother's Day / יום המשפחה, bar or bat mitzvah) and lower the emotional risk.
7. Seed the first posts with public-domain Israeli archive photos (section 6) until real customer content exists, and say clearly that they are demos.

- No reliable aggregate view counts for the 2024 AI hug trend were found.

## 2. Reaction videos: how businesses collect them ethically, and how well they perform

### Takeaway
Reaction videos of the recipient (a grandmother, a parent, a widow) are the most emotionally powerful content in this niche, and the documented viral examples are almost all family-filmed, not business-filmed. Collect them as user-generated content: ask the gift-giver to film, get written permission for each specific use, never make an incentive depend on the reaction being positive, and disclose any incentive.

### Cited Findings
- A grandson showed his 100-year-old grandmother an AI video of her husband, who had died more than 30 years earlier. Her reaction went viral and was covered by mainstream news. — [CTV News (search summary; fetch blocked)](https://www.ctvnews.ca/sci-tech/article/100-year-old-woman-reacts-to-ai-generated-video-of-late-husband/)
- "He's Still Here": a grandmother's tears on seeing her late husband in an AI video were posted on Instagram and picked up by regional media. — [SAYS.com](https://says.com/my/tech/grandma-reacts-ai-video-late-husband)
- The Lakyn Bowman grandmother reaction (8.5M views) had to be posted without its original audio because the recording didn't save. Reactions happen once, so setup matters. — [Bored Panda](https://www.boredpanda.com/surprise-grandma-recreating-photos-tiktok/)
- In the classic Deep Nostalgia example, a TikToker filmed her parents watching an animation of a grandfather, and commenters said they "sobbed" after trying it themselves. Reactions push viewers to try it, which turns into demand. — [Yahoo AU](https://au.finance.yahoo.com/news/parents-viral-reaction-photo-app-214801554.html)
- Permission practice: get written consent that covers the planned use. A permission to repost on organic social should not be stretched to cover paid ads, product pages, email or broadcast. Check that the person granting permission has authority over the video, and clear music and any other people who appear in it separately. — [CapCut customer-video rights framework (vendor)](https://www.capcut.com/create/customer-video-rights-reposting-framework); [U.S. Chamber of Commerce](https://www.uschamber.com/co/grow/marketing/how-user-generated-content-drives-sales)
- Incentives: a business cannot tie an incentive to a particular sentiment (for example, a discount only for five-star reviews or positive videos), and incentivized content must be disclosed. — [Fit Small Business on FTC rules](https://fitsmallbusiness.com/sales-marketing/ftc-rules-customer-content/)
- Small businesses usually only need a modest incentive, such as a discount or a drawing entry, and friendly requests ("wall of love") work better than pressure. — [Clipchamp](https://clipchamp.com/en/blog/user-generated-videos-small-business-marketing/); [Testimonial.to](https://testimonial.to/resources/how-to-get-user-generated-content)
- Israeli case law: consent to a photo being used in one context does not extend to wider advertising use. A business that used a customer's photo in a national campaign beyond the agreed scope was held to have violated privacy. — [Kol Zchut](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/שימוש_בתמונה_של_אדם_במסע_פרסום_מעבר_להסכמתו_מהווה_פגיעה_בפרטיות)
- The Israeli Privacy Protection Authority stresses that consent must be informed ("הסכמה מדעת"). — [gov.il Privacy rights guide](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/generalpage/files/he/Rights.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Collection workflow.** (a) At delivery, send the gift-giver a short "how to film the moment" guide: phone horizontal or vertical on a stand, filming before the reveal, sound on, recipient's face visible. (b) After the moment, ask, without pressure, "would you be willing to share it?" (c) Get a WhatsApp or written consent that names the platforms (IG, TikTok, FB, website), says whether paid ads are included (separate tick box), whether faces can be shown or should be blurred, and confirms the recipient also agrees. The elderly person on camera is a separate rights-holder from the gift-giver. (d) Offer a disclosed thank-you that is the same whether or not the reaction is "good", such as 20% off the next order or a free extra animation, and say on the post that the family received a thank-you gift if the post was incentivized.
- Never stage or re-enact reactions with actors or friends presented as customers. That is a fake testimonial (section 6).
- Treat grief reactions (widows, recent bereavement) more carefully: ask again after a few days, offer a version without faces (hands holding the phone, the screen only) and a version with text only.
- Before customers exist, the owner can film her own family's reaction to her own family photo. This is an authentic "founder story" ("I made this for my grandmother, and this is why I started Zikaron Chai") and needs no third-party consent beyond her own relatives.

### Gaps
- I found no quantitative data (e.g. engagement rate versus other formats) for reaction videos specifically in the restoration niche. Performance evidence is anecdotal virality.
- No Israeli template release form for UGC videos was found. A lawyer-reviewed Hebrew consent text is recommended.

## 3. Giveaways and "free for the first N" campaigns: effectiveness and rules (Meta/TikTok, and Israeli law)

### Takeaway
"Free for the first N" (first-come, or chosen by story) is the lowest-risk launch mechanic in Israel because it has no element of chance. Random draws ("הגרלה") fall under the Penal Law's lottery prohibition and need to operate under the Finance Ministry's general permit for advertising lotteries, with written rules (תקנון). On Meta, do not require tagging, sharing or likes as entry mechanics. They breach promotion guidelines or engagement-bait rules and get distribution cut.

### Cited Findings
**Israeli law**
- The Penal Law 1977 (sections 224-228, "סימן י"ב") prohibits organizing or running a forbidden game, lottery or bet, and section 228 also prohibits publishing a notice about a lottery. The Finance Minister (or his delegate) may grant a permit in advance for a type of lottery or a specific one, published in Reshumot. — [Knesset Research and Information Center](https://fs.knesset.gov.il/globaldocs/MMM/e576fbce-a6d5-ec11-8147-005056aac6c3/2_e576fbce-a6d5-ec11-8147-005056aac6c3_11_19645.pdf); [Efraty CPA firm on lottery supervision](https://www.efraty.com/?p=4077)
- Israeli brands' Instagram giveaway rules state that the draw runs "under the general permit for running lotteries for commercial advertising under the Penal Law 1977". Some use an examining committee with a legal representative. — [Ivory Instagram contest rules 2026](https://www.ivory.co.il/files/Manual/instagram-contest-2026.pdf); [Samsung Israel lucky-draw T&C](https://images.samsung.com/is/content/samsung/assets/il/offer/tnc/lucky-draw-tnc.pdf)
- The Finance Ministry Director-General (named in the report as Israel Malachi) reportedly signed a new general permit for advertising lotteries, described as including easing for small businesses (for example, an exemption from an external supervisor). The full conditions were not retrievable. — [Funder](https://funder.co.il/article/214059)
- The Attorney General's position on Instagram advertising: a tag or hashtag naming the sponsor is not enough, and the post must state at the start that it is advertising or sponsored. — [Calcalist Tech](https://calcalistech.com/ctech/articles/0,7340,L-3889014,00.html); [Srugim](https://www.srugim.co.il/529449-היועמש-אושיות-אינסטגרם-ידווחו-על-פרסו)
- For comparison: in the UK, a social-media lottery needs registration or a licence. — [UK Gambling Commission](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/public-and-players/guide/page/fundraising-and-lotteries-on-social-media)

**Platform rules**
- The host is responsible for running the promotion lawfully. It must publish official rules, include a full release of Instagram by each entrant, state that Instagram doesn't sponsor or run the promotion, and give eligibility terms such as age and country. Inaccurate tagging, or encouraging it, is prohibited. — [Instagram Promotion Guidelines](https://help.instagram.com/179379842258600); [Gleam summary](https://gleam.io/blog/instagram-giveaway-rules/)
- On Facebook, requiring friend tags (as a condition or for extra entries) or shares to a personal timeline is not allowed. Sources disagree on whether follows and likes may be rewarded, because Meta's spam policy bars exchanging anything for engagement. — [ShortStack](https://www.shortstack.com/help/facebook-promotion-guidelines/); [Gleam](https://gleam.io/blog/instagram-giveaway-rules/)
- Meta has demoted "engagement bait" since Dec 2017, including tag bait ("tag a friend who..."), comment bait, share bait, vote bait and react bait. Pages that do it repeatedly see bigger reach drops. It is now part of Meta's Content Distribution Guidelines. — [Meta Newsroom, Dec 2017](https://about.fb.com/news/2017/12/news-feed-fyi-fighting-engagement-bait-on-facebook/); [Meta Transparency Center: Engagement Bait](https://transparency.meta.com/features/approach-to-ranking/content-distribution-guidelines/engagement-bait/)
- If another brand provides the prize or pays for the giveaway, Meta treats the post as branded content and disclosure rules apply. — [Gleam](https://gleam.io/blog/instagram-giveaway-rules/)

### Inferences
- **Recommended launch mechanic: "10 free memories for the first 10 stories".** Ask people to send (by DM or a form, not by public comment) a photo plus two sentences about the person in it. Pick by story, not at random, so there is no chance element. In return, ask optionally (not as a condition) for permission to share the result and reaction. This avoids the lottery regime, avoids engagement-bait demotion, and produces the first portfolio and testimonials at once. Publish short written rules anyway: dates, number of winners, how they are chosen, what is free (e.g. the 49 ILS tier), and consent terms.
- A random draw is possible but adds compliance work (written rules referring to the general permit, record-keeping), so it is better avoided for a one-person business unless a lawyer confirms the new small-business easing.
- Prefer "send me a DM" or "link in bio" over "comment X to win", "tag 3 friends" or "share to your story to enter".
- The free watermarked preview is itself the strongest organic offer. Posts can invite people to "send a photo and get a free preview within 24 hours", which is a service offer, not a contest.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the text of the current general permit for advertising lotteries (date, prize-value caps, small-business conditions), and could not confirm whether a no-purchase draw where entry is a like or tag counts as "consideration" (תמורה) under Israeli law. Legal advice needed.
- No data on conversion rates of "free for first N" campaigns for service micro-businesses was found.
- TikTok's own promotion/contest rules were not retrieved.

## 4. Collaborations: micro-influencers, genealogy/heritage creators, event vendors, nursing homes, memorial services, school roots projects

### Takeaway
For a business with zero budget, the best-supported partnerships are (a) nano and micro creators, who have higher engagement than big accounts on both Instagram and TikTok and will often swap content for a free product, (b) genealogy and heritage communities (Facebook groups and societies), where the need for restoration is already explicit, and (c) the Israeli 7th-grade "roots project" (עבודת שורשים) and bar/bat mitzvah ecosystem, which already asks families to collect and present old photos. Evidence for nursing homes, event DJs and memorial services is anecdotal or inferred.

### Cited Findings
- Influencer Marketing Hub's 2025 figures (via a secondary source) put Instagram micro-influencers at 3.86% average engagement versus 1.21% for mega-influencers. HypeAuditor's 2026 data (via eMarketer) puts Instagram nano (1K-10K) at 1.78% and micro (10K-50K) at 0.54%. Methodologies differ, but the direction is consistent: smaller accounts get more engagement per follower on Instagram. — [archive.com (secondary)](https://archive.com/blog/micro-influencer-engagement-rate-statistics); [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/exclusive-instagram-s-nano-influencers-outperform-as-do-tiktok-s-upper-echelon-of-creators)
- On TikTok, Influencer Marketing Hub's 2024 figures put nano at 10.3%, micro at 8.7% and mid-tier at 7.5%. eMarketer notes that on TikTok, larger followings don't necessarily dilute engagement, unlike Instagram. — [NetInfluencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/tiktok-nano-influencer-engagement-far-outpaces-other-platforms-per-report/); [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/exclusive-instagram-s-nano-influencers-outperform-as-do-tiktok-s-upper-echelon-of-creators)
- Stack Influence estimates a much smaller micro/macro gap on Instagram (about 0.7% vs 0.6%). — [Stack Influence](https://stackinfluence.com/the-average-influencer-engagement-rate-in-2025/)
- Genealogy researchers, families restoring heirlooms, local history groups and photographers are listed as core clients, and marketing via genealogy societies, family-history conferences, Facebook groups and local historical societies is recommended. Marketing to the general public is noted as harder. — [Small business ideas guide](https://smallbusinessideas.pages.dev/ideas/archive-document-archival-services-photo-restoration-preservation.html); [GrowthScribe](https://growthscribe.com/turn-photo-restoration-into-a-profitable-business/)
- One volunteer restorer turned a Facebook restoration group into a paid business, with prices listed alongside links to her site. A dedicated community (PhotoVideoFix) was created to connect people who need fixes with fixers. — [Genealogy blog, 2019](https://genealogyalibraryofmemories.blogspot.com/2019/07/what-should-i-do-with-old-family-photos.html); [PhotoVideoFix launch](https://payhip.com/Aebe/blog/news/launching-the-new-facebook-community-photovideofix)
- Israeli school roots-project guidelines ask students to collect testimony from relatives and visual material (photos, recordings, videos), and to add personal photos, certificates and keepsakes. — [Makif school roots-project booklet](https://makifh.schooly.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/חוברת-הנחיות-לכתיבת-עבודת-שורשים.pdf)

- The Ministry of Education has a 7th-grade teaching kit "Roots in Visual Art" built around the family album, and an intergenerational program that documents a grandparent's story with old photos and recordings. — [Ministry of Education kit](https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/Mazkirut_Pedagogit/Omanut/shorashimbaomanutfinal.pdf); [Ministry of Education intergenerational program](https://meyda.education.gov.il/files/Mazkirut_Pedagogit/MikzootAzmaeem/Beshvili_morshet_saba_vesavta_mishtafim.pdf)
- Israeli spam law allows a one-time approach to a business (בית עסק) asking for its consent to receive advertising. This is the legal route for cold B2B outreach to event planners, photographers, nursing homes or burial societies by email or WhatsApp. — [Knesset document](https://fs.knesset.gov.il/globaldocs/MMM/63275fbe-180e-e711-80cc-00155d0206a2/2_63275fbe-180e-e711-80cc-00155d0206a2_11_10750.pdf); [Israel Chamber of Commerce](https://www.chamber.org.il/serviceslobby/legal/74023/115485/)

### Inferences
Partnership plan suited to one person and no budget:
- **Nano creators (1K-10K) in "family", "savta", "olim stories" and pet niches.** Offer one free restoration plus animation of their own family photo in exchange for an honest post, disclosed as "פרסומת / שיתוף פעולה" at the start of the post per the Attorney General's position. A product gift counts as a material connection.
- **Genealogy and heritage communities.** Israeli and diaspora Jewish genealogy Facebook groups, the Israel Genealogical Society, and "שורשים" groups. Lead with value (free tips on scanning and preserving photos, a free restoration of one "mystery photo" a month), then mention the service where group rules allow. Read each group's rules on promotion first.
- **Roots project and bar/bat mitzvah package.** A "פרויקט שורשים חי" package: restore and animate 3-5 family photos for the 7th-grade roots project or for a bar/bat mitzvah slideshow. Reach parents through parent WhatsApp groups only via members who choose to share it (not bulk messages), and pitch bar/bat mitzvah DJs, event producers and photographers with the one-time-approach-to-business exception, offering them a referral fee or a free demo for their showreel.
- **Weddings and anniversaries.** Animated photos of the couple's grandparents' weddings for the wedding-day video, offered through wedding photographers and videographers.
- **Nursing homes and senior day centers.** A free "memory morning" where residents' photos are restored and shown. This gives authentic reaction content and word of mouth with adult children, but needs explicit consent from residents (and from guardians where relevant) before anything is posted.
- **Memorial services (חברות קדישא, memorial-booklet printers, gravestone makers).** Higher sensitivity. Offer restoration only (not animation) by default, with no social posting.

### Gaps
- No case studies were found of restoration/animation businesses partnering with nursing homes, DJs, event planners or memorial services, and no evidence was found on their conversion. These recommendations are inferred.
- No Israel-specific micro-influencer pricing or engagement benchmarks were found.
- I did not verify current membership numbers or promotion rules of Israeli genealogy Facebook groups.

## 5. Ethics and backlash risks: "creepy" or uncanny animations of the dead, grief sensitivity, AI disclosure

### Takeaway
Every major viral moment in this niche (Deep Nostalgia, the AI hug, Ohanian, the Gemini polaroids) drew an "it's creepy / unhealthy / fake memories" backlash next to the tears. In Israel, the most dangerous area is Holocaust-related content: the Auschwitz Museum and historians have publicly condemned AI-generated "victim" images and emotional fictionalized Holocaust stories as distortion. Mitigate with consent-first, subtle-motion, clearly labeled work, and never use real victims or strangers as marketing props.

### Cited Findings
- MyHeritage itself acknowledged "some people love the Deep Nostalgia feature and consider it magical, while others find it creepy". Reviewers found loved ones sometimes reanimated into "some vaguely familiar CGI figure". — [Yahoo UK](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/deep-nostalgia-creepy-uses-ai-130130073.html); [Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/culture/tv-radio-web/the-creepy-deepfakes-anyone-can-create-how-deep-nostalgia-animates-your-family-photos-1.4498990)

Data handling of family photos is a trust issue. — [Yahoo UK](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/deep-nostalgia-creepy-uses-ai-130130073.html)

### Inferences
Guardrails that also work as marketing (trust is part of the brand):
- **Subtle motion is the default.** Blink, breath, slight smile, head turn. Avoid exaggerated expressions, speech or lip-sync of the dead, invented hugs between people who never met, and anything that changes identity. Offer "restoration only" as a dignified option.
- **Consent from the family, always.** Only animate photos supplied by family members, and never post a deceased person's animation without the supplying relative's explicit consent. Never use strangers' or celebrities' dead relatives as examples.
- **Holocaust and fallen soldiers (חללי צה"ל, נפגעי טרור).** Do not use them as marketing demos. Do not invent "victims" or animate archive photos of victims for content. Do not run sales or "trend" posts on Yom HaShoah or Yom HaZikaron. If a customer orders a survivor's or fallen soldier's photo, treat it privately and post nothing unless the family explicitly asks to share. Expect public sensitivity after Oct 7: the risk of hurting families and drawing backlash is high.
- **Honest framing.** Say "an AI animation inspired by the photo", not "see her really move again". Reply to "this is creepy" comments with empathy, not argument.
- **Grief-aware sales.** No countdown or scarcity pressure on bereaved customers. Keep the free preview and a clear "no obligation" message.
- **Data trust.** Say plainly how long uploaded photos are kept and when they are deleted. The Deep Nostalgia privacy criticism shows this matters.

- No academic or clinical study on the psychological effects of animated photos of the deceased was retrieved. Only journalistic framing was found.
- No statement from Yad Vashem specifically about AI animation of family photos was found (only Auschwitz Museum statements).

## 6. Legal and policy pitfalls: fake reviews and testimonials, spam law, engagement bait and group spam, public-domain vs copyrighted demos, privacy and consent, AI labels

### Takeaway
The main legal risks for a one-person Israeli business are: (1) WhatsApp, SMS or email marketing without prior consent (statutory damages of up to 1,000 ILS per message, without proof of harm), (2) fake or incentivized-positive reviews and testimonials (deception under the Consumer Protection Law, and for US customers the FTC's 2024 rule with civil penalties), (3) posting customers' photos beyond the consent given, and (4) skipping AI labels on photorealistic animated video on Meta and TikTok. Demo content should come from genuinely public-domain archive photos with each license checked.

### Cited Findings
**Israeli spam law (סעיף 30א לחוק התקשורת)**
- Sending an advertisement ("דבר פרסומת") by SMS, fax, automated dialing, email or any electronic messaging requires the recipient's prior explicit consent. A court may award up to 1,000 ILS per advertisement without proof of harm. Liability is higher if the sender keeps going after a refusal, or if the message gives no way to opt out. — [Kol Zchut](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/פיצוי_בגין_משלוח_דברי_פרסומת_ללא_הסכמה_של_הנמען_(חוק_הספאם)); [Protocol law firm](https://protocol.co.il/spam-law/)
- The law covers WhatsApp messages as "electronic messages", per a Times of Israel blog by a lawyer. It covers the same 1,000 ILS per message, and criminal exposure exists. — [Times of Israel blog: "The law your WhatsApp bot keeps breaking"](https://blogs.timesofisrael.com/the-law-your-whatsapp-bot-keeps-breaking/)
- Exceptions: (a) existing customers who gave their details during a purchase, were offered the chance to refuse ads and did not, and only for similar products (narrowly construed by the Supreme Court), and (b) a one-time approach to a business asking for consent. Anyone who withdraws consent must be removed. — [Israel Chamber of Commerce](https://www.chamber.org.il/serviceslobby/legal/74023/115485/); [Mako](https://www.mako.co.il/nexter-news/Article-76c7be38e01e781027.htm); [Knesset document](https://fs.knesset.gov.il/globaldocs/MMM/63275fbe-180e-e711-80cc-00155d0206a2/2_63275fbe-180e-e711-80cc-00155d0206a2_11_10750.pdf)

**Fake reviews and testimonials**
- Israel: the Consumer Protection Authority's draft supervisor guidance (Jan 2021) says that writing or publishing fake reviews (attributed to non-existent consumers or to people who never used the product or service, including via bloggers or influencers) is deception under section 2 of the Consumer Protection Law. It also flags hiding negative reviews and promising incentives for positive reviews, and recommends publishing reviews in full with dates. — [gov.il draft guidance (PDF)](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/news/cpfta_internetopinion2021/he/docs_draft_supervisor_guide.pdf)
- The Authority's annual report also raised businesses deleting negative reviews on social networks. — [Consumer Protection Authority 2022 annual report](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/pfta_annual_reports/he/%D7%93%D7%95%D7%97%20%D7%A9%D7%A0%D7%AA%D7%99%202022%20%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%A4%D7%99.pdf)
- Ynet reported that lawsuits over online consumer reviews have doubled. — [Ynet](https://www.ynet.co.il/economy/article/SyzEMeG0w); see also [Globes on fake reviews](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001281530)
- US/diaspora: the FTC's Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials was announced on 14 Aug 2024 and took effect on 21 Oct 2024. It bans fake or false reviews and testimonials (including AI-generated ones), buying positive or negative reviews, undisclosed insider reviews, company-controlled "independent" review sites, review suppression, and fake social-media indicators (bought followers or views). Civil penalties for knowing violations were up to $51,744 per violation (2024 figure, adjusted annually for inflation). — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials); [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/08/22/2024-18519/trade-regulation-rule-on-the-use-of-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials); [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://dwt.com/insights/2024/08/ftc-finalizes-rule-banning-fake-consumer-reviews); [Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/ftc-finalizes-rule-on-fake-reviews)

**AI labeling on platforms**
- Meta: Facebook, Instagram and Threads may require an AI label on content with photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio that was digitally created or altered, including by AI. The requirement does not apply to still images. Meta may apply penalties for failing to disclose, and may auto-label content as "AI info" based on detected signals. — [Meta Help Center](https://www.meta.com/help/artificial-intelligence/1783222608822690/); [Meta 2024 proxy statement (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000132680124000034/meta-20240418.htm); [NPR, Feb 2024](https://www.npr.org/2024/02/06/1229317971/meta-labeling-ai-generated-images-instagram-facebook-artificial-intelligence)
- TikTok: creators must label all AI-generated content that contains realistic images, audio or video. TikTok may apply the "AI-generated" label automatically, and since May 2024 does so for content carrying C2PA content credentials from other tools. — [TikTok Support](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/ai-generated-content); [TikTok Transparency](https://www.tiktok.com/transparency/en/supporting-responsible-transparent-ai-generated-content/); [MacRumors, May 2024](https://www.macrumors.com/2024/05/10/tiktok-label-ai-generated-content)

**Engagement bait and group spam**
- Meta demotes engagement bait (tag, comment, share, vote and react bait). Pages that use it repeatedly suffer larger reach drops. — [Meta Newsroom 2017](https://about.fb.com/news/2017/12/news-feed-fyi-fighting-engagement-bait-on-facebook/); [Meta Transparency Center](https://transparency.meta.com/features/approach-to-ranking/content-distribution-guidelines/engagement-bait/)

**Privacy and consent when posting customers' photos (Israel)**
- Section 1 of the Privacy Protection Law prohibits infringing privacy without consent, and the consent must be informed. — [gov.il Privacy Authority rights guide](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/generalpage/files/he/Rights.pdf)
- Consent limited to a specific use does not cover broader advertising use. A court held that using a customer's photo in a national campaign beyond the agreed scope infringed privacy. — [Kol Zchut](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/שימוש_בתמונה_של_אדם_במסע_פרסום_מעבר_להסכמתו_מהווה_פגיעה_בפרטיות)
- Israeli courts have heard claims over businesses posting a person's photos on Instagram and Facebook for marketing without consent. — [Bizportal / Takdin](https://www.bizportal.co.il/takdin/news/article/20013373)

**Public-domain vs copyrighted demo photos**
- The National Library of Israel released about 50,000 Meitar Collection photos (roughly 1948-1969) to Wikimedia Commons for free use without permission requests. — [Ynet](https://www.ynet.co.il/news/article/sy00uixtsf); [NLI Meitar Collection](https://www.nli.org.il/en/discover/photos/collections/meitar-collection)
- Wikimedia released about 28,000 "public domain" photos from Israeli archives, including pre-1948 images, but the Central Zionist Archives disputed this and said some were copyrighted. A "PD" tag is not a guarantee. — [Calcalist Tech](https://calcalistech.com/ctech/articles/0,7340,L-3749822,00.html)
- Wikimedia file pages citing the Israeli Copyright Act 2007 say government-created photos without a special arrangement enter the public domain on 1 January of the 51st year after creation (secondary source). The Government Press Office's National Photo Collection is a common source. — [Wikipedia: National Photo Collection (Israel)](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Photo_Collection_(Israel))

### Inferences
Compliance checklist for Zikaron Chai:
- **WhatsApp/SMS/email.** Message only people who asked (a website form with an unticked consent checkbox, or people who wrote first). Include "להסרה השיבו 'הסר'" in every marketing message. Keep consent records. Never add people to broadcast lists or groups without consent. Use the existing-customer exception only for similar offers, with an opt-out offered at purchase. For B2B, one consent-seeking message per business. Personal one-to-one messages from the owner to friends asking them to share probably carry less risk than a bulk send, but a bulk "new business!" broadcast to the owner's whole contact list is the classic violation.
- **Reviews and testimonials.** No invented testimonials, no "beta" reviews from friends presented as customers, no AI-written reviews, no deleting negative reviews, and no review incentives that depend on the rating. Label demo videos made from archive or own-family photos as demos ("דוגמה"), not as customer stories. Diaspora (US) customers fall under the FTC rule.
- **AI disclosure.** Turn on the "AI info" / "AI-generated" toggle on every animated Reel or TikTok, and say "אנימציה בבינה מלאכותית" in the caption. Disclosure is also an ethical trust signal (section 5).
- **Customer photos.** Use a written, scoped consent covering platforms, ads, faces, the deceased person's image, and withdrawal. The default is private delivery. Post only with an explicit opt-in.
- **Demo photos.** Use NLI and Meitar or GPO photos whose license is verified one by one, record the source and license, and avoid photos of identifiable Holocaust victims or fallen soldiers even when they are PD (section 5).
- **Paid collaborations.** Disclose at the start of the post ("פרסומת" / "שיתוף פעולה בתשלום / קיבלתי במתנה"), per the Attorney General's position.
